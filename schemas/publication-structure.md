# Publication structure — US comic planning options

Companion guidance for `comic-production-standard-v0.1-draft`. Source: creator-supplied “Aufbau eines US-Comics (Standard: 32 Seiten)” notes, 2026-09-16. These are editorial planning options, not independently verified market statistics, universal publishing requirements or legal guidance.

## Cover terminology and functions

U1–U4 are German-facing aliases for existing stable C1–C4 slot IDs. Do not duplicate or renumber physical slots when changing display language.

| Display alias | Stable ID | Position/function | Suggested content |
|---|---|---|---|
| U1 / Front Cover | C1 | Front outside | Main artwork, series logo, issue number, price, barcode and artist credits |
| U2 / Inside Front Cover | C2 | Front inside | Indicia/imprint or advertising |
| U3 / Inside Back Cover | C3 | Back inside | Advertising, merchandise/store promotion or Next Issue preview |
| U4 / Back Cover | C4 | Back outside | Full-page third-party advertising candidate |

The creator's notes prioritize U4 commercially. This framework records that placement preference without claiming a guaranteed price, market value or mandatory third-party advertiser.

For each issue, record a cover-content plan: slot, chosen function, content/assets, unresolved items, and acceptance reference. For U1 explicitly resolve artwork, logo, issue number, price, barcode and credits as supplied, OPEN or intentionally omitted. Never invent a price, barcode, publisher identity or contributor credit. Imprint content is supplied/confirmed by the publisher; listing a location does not establish legal sufficiency.

Cover function and physical type are distinct: a cover with an imprint, advertisement or preview remains one `cover` page. Imprint and full-page advertising are alternative uses of U2 unless an explicit shared-area plan allocates non-overlapping rectangles. The same applies to preview and advertising on U3. No silent double booking. Page counts and ad inventory must be updated together when a choice changes.

## Story, splash and credits

A splash is a single dominant image on one story page. An opening splash may also carry issue title and creative-team credits, but an opening splash is optional and splash imagery can occur elsewhere. Credits over a splash do not consume a second physical or editorial page. Story owns the narrative image/reading intent; Production reserves lettering/credit areas and Design supplies visual identity.

A double-page splash occupies two facing physical story slots, not one. Give both the same spread ID, list the shared panel ID, designate one owning side and reference it on the other. One cross-gutter image counts as one unique story panel: owner side has local count 1, reference side has local count 0 with an explicit shared-panel reference. The reference side is not a blank page. This is the only exception to the positive local panel count for story-bearing pages; an unexplained zero remains invalid. For a left-to-right bound issue with C1 at position 1, pair even position with the next odd position. Keep vital text and credits away from the gutter as well as inside the configured safe area.

Do not turn an existing multi-panel opening into a splash automatically; propose the panel/budget change and reconcile it with Story.

## Advertising categories

In addition to size and placement, an issue may record `advertising_kind`: `house`, `third_party` or `OPEN`; advertiser/product or OPEN; purpose; creative reference; and acceptance reference. `house` includes publisher promotions, merchandise and other own offers. A Next Issue page can be editorial or house advertising: choose its primary purpose explicitly. An editorial preview is not counted as an ad unless intentionally classified as such. Existing reservations without the field are unresolved, not implicitly third-party.

Interior ads can be interleaved with Story, but reserve specific slots and check pacing, facing pages and reveal turns. Do not assume a target advertising range authorizes extra pages or overrides the creator's chosen inventory.

## Editorial matter

Optional contents include an editorial, reader letters (Lettercol), publisher highlights, afterword, credits, imprint and a Next Issue preview. Locate each in a specific cover/interior slot and track source/readiness. A reserved letters page does not authorize fabricated reader letters. The 1–2-page range from the supplied notes is a starting point, not a mandatory allocation; credits or an imprint can share another page when explicitly laid out.

## Reconciling the supplied page ranges

The notes suggest 20–24 Story pages, 6–8 ad pages and 1–2 editorial pages. Their accounting basis is unspecified: if all ads are interior, adding four covers gives 31–38 total pages. These ranges cannot be independent mandatory quotas in a 32-page product. If ad totals include cover ads, those cover sides must not be counted twice.

Under this framework, a “32-page issue” always means 32 total sides including four covers, leaving 28 interiors. “32 interiors plus covers” means 36 total. Record this explicitly in intake.

Two possible 32-total budgets (illustrations, not added defaults):

| Variant | Covers | Story | Interior ads | Interior editorial | Total | Optional cover ads | Ad-bearing sides overall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| More Story | 4 | 22 | 4 | 2 | 32 | 2 (U3/U4; U2 imprint) | 6 |
| More interior ads | 4 | 20 | 6 | 2 | 32 | 2 (U3/U4; U2 imprint) | 8 |

Here U2 contains the imprint; the two interior editorial pages serve other functions. An opening splash and its credits are included within the Story count. These budget illustrations have no physical page map yet; a full plan must add one before use.

The existing 24-page example remains valid: 4 covers + 16 Story + 2 interior ads + 2 editorial. It is a compact issue and does not have to meet the suggested 20–24 Story-page range. For its own material, each project selects format, extent, advertising inventory and editorial functions independently.
